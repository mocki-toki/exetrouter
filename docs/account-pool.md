# OAuth account pool

Each active ChatGPT OAuth account has independent encrypted credentials, generation, refresh lease, model catalog, quota observations and operation health. Adding the same upstream account updates its record instead of creating another pool entry.

## Selection

The visible model list is a union of active account catalogs. An independent full-context request chooses an eligible account for its model, respecting current credentials/catalog, cooldown, operational pauses, quota observations and active load. Explicit cache/session hints provide a stable soft preference among eligible accounts. Stale quota is not treated as confirmed current capacity; percentages are not combined across subscriptions.

HTTP text/tool requests can choose independently. Known user-scoped opaque context and a bound WebSocket retain account affinity during normal operation. Confirmed quota cooldown or a current 100% window with a future reset permits automatic account failover when another eligible account exists. Automatic ranking governs eligible alternatives; exhausted subscriptions are skipped when other capacity is available. If every subscription is at 100% without explicit cooldown, upstream credits may still permit a request on the original account. Stale observations never trigger transfer; fresh urgency may permit a safe soft transfer under the rules below.

## Availability and automatic selection

`exrd admin oauth disable ID` (or `oauth policy ID --enabled false`) excludes an account for everyone without deleting it or changing OAuth credentials. `oauth enable ID` reverses this. Personal activation controls and the `account_set` management action are removed; historical user preferences are retained only for rollback and never read by routing. Operator policy and lock markers remain intact. Already submitted responses finish. The next owned continuation moves to an eligible active account when its complete current context is available, including incremental WS history recovered from the latest completed window. This also applies to standalone OAuth deactivation (`exr account disable ID`, reversed by reauthorization). Disabled credentials are never used for the replacement. Saved backend conversations cannot transfer; unavailable WS recovery requires reconnecting with full context. Without an eligible replacement, the request fails before inference.

Manual priorities and switching rules are removed from CLI/TUI and rejected by management, including old wire requests. Historical database overrides are retained only for rollback, never read by selection. Routing is identical in standalone and server mode.

### Ranking and switching

- Accounts enter urgent mode when a supported available reset expires in **at most 72 hours**, or a current weekly window with usable remaining quota resets in **at most 24 hours**. The earliest deadline wins within the urgent group. Their automatic switching threshold remains **0% remaining**.
- Within an equal urgent deadline, prefer the earlier expiring credit, then more observed credits expiring within 72 hours. Strategic inventory and its expiry never improve an account's ranking; incomplete inventory is not extrapolated from the total count.
- Otherwise, use accounts with a short window first; weekly-only accounts are reserves. Window presence comes from fresh, complete live limits, not plan names or primary/secondary position. This includes actual 5-hour, 8-hour and other reported short durations. Unknown window structure remains unknown and ranks after known usable subscription classes.
- Outside urgent mode, new independent dialogs within the best eligible window class are distributed with weight **remaining weekly percentage / days until weekly reset**. Fresh, complete weekly observations are required for every candidate in that tier. Weighted cache affinity gives a stable preference for the same hint while candidates and weights are unchanged; requests without hints use weighted random selection. These are approximate shares of admissions, not guaranteed shares of tokens or spend: dialog sizes vary and percentages are not calibrated absolute capacities across plans. Weekly-only accounts remain reserves behind usable short-window accounts. Pacing weight changes alone never transfer an owned dialog.
- All accounts use quota through **0% remaining**. There is no 1% rotation reserve. Exhausted accounts are skipped when other usable capacity exists; if all are exhausted without a confirmed rejection, retain the existing credit-backed fallback. Economic ranking precedes soft cache affinity. In urgent mode, or when pacing metadata is incomplete, retain the existing cache preference; without one, prefer greater observed usage when every candidate's usage is known, then active load and stable account ID. Unknown quota never receives an invented weight.

A bounded runtime worker refreshes live usage and reset inventory independently of the dashboard. Metadata freshness is 60 seconds. Stale observations cannot create urgent mode, prove absence of a short window or authorize a reset. Unknown capacity is not zero. Diagnostics show availability, automatic reason/deadline/window class, threshold and the most recent reset outcome. The dashboard marks enabled urgent accounts with a red `Priority` label and omits the ordinary reserve label.

Account details also show [hourly subscription history](quota-history.md): short
and weekly windows share one seven-day chart with distinct colors and hourly
values. Collection is bounded numeric metadata, independent of dashboards and
token counts; missing observations remain gaps. Schema 14 requires the reviewed
migration/rollback and history starts only after the server upgrade.

The green `Best` marker is a server-wide recommendation, identical for every viewer at the same observation time. It uses shared account quotas, deadlines, health and operator availability, never personal preferences or the viewer's client count. It recommends the account with the greatest target share in the best eligible general-meter tier (stable ID breaks equal weights). During urgency it identifies the best unhinted candidate. It is not a global active-account switch: model support, cache hints and owned dialogs can choose differently. No marker is shown when the top tier's quota/window metadata is unknown or stale, or when every account is exhausted/unavailable. The recommendation does not submit inference. Target shares remain unknown for tied non-paced candidates, where cache affinity may choose any of them.

Account details show server-wide `Clients`, counting **all users' unique API tokens** with an admitted inference request on that account in the last 15 minutes, plus tokens with unfinished requests regardless of age. Each viewer sees their own token IDs and locally decrypted names. Other users' clients expose only stable domain-separated HMAC aliases, last-request times and running-request counts: neither raw token IDs, names, ciphertext nor user identities are returned. Aliases survive restarts with the existing server signing key and match across accounts. This intentionally shares pseudonymous activity, not token-management access or user-scoped usage details. The last-request time is per account, not token authentication activity. Idle WebSockets, pings and metadata calls do not renew presence. One token may represent multiple applications and may appear on multiple accounts. Lists are bounded to 128 entries, with unfinished requests first and the exact global total count reported. Activity snapshots refresh every 30 seconds, including while account details are open; inactivity only hides rows and never removes history or affinity. Startup recovery finalizes abandoned durable admissions as unknown so they do not remain running clients. `clients.scope: "server"` and `capabilities.global_account_clients: 1` distinguish these snapshots from older user-scoped servers.

An expiring reset is consumed automatically when its expiry is **less than 72 hours away** and weekly quota is **at most 1% remaining**. Other subscriptions need not be exhausted: the purpose is to spend expiring resets instead of reserve subscriptions. Longer-lived strategic resets are never spent automatically. During the final **24 hours**, consumed current ordinary quota permits one early salvage for the already-expiring cohort, even above 1% weekly remaining; a fully unused account is not reset. Until that cohort's latest expiry, further credits require new weekly depletion to ≤1% after verified recovery. Small subsequent usage cannot trigger a cascade of early resets. A short-window exhaustion alone does not consume a 24–72-hour reset. See [automatic reset safety](cli.md#reset-credits).

An owned continuation normally retains affinity. Explicit deactivation, exhaustion or a strictly better economic tier may trigger a transfer only with complete recoverable current context and an eligible alternative. This includes returning from a weekly-only reserve when short-window capacity recovers. Proactive alternatives require fresh quota; the final choice is rechecked against the original tier. Same-tier quota/load differences do not move owned sessions. Otherwise the healthy original continues, including saved conversations and unrecoverable WS branches. A refused proactive replacement handshake falls back before generation submission; a deactivated original is never a fallback. Preserve downstream WS/scoped identities, discard account-specific turn state and preserve concurrent forks. No economic preference bypasses authentication, operational backoff or replacement-account availability.

Doctor/Limits advertise `capabilities.automatic_account_routing: 1` and `automatic_reset_credits: 1`, not the retired routing-rules capability. Rows expose operator-owned `preference.enabled/locked`, `routing`, `threshold_reached` and `automatic_reset.status`. Old clients may inspect metadata, but personal availability mutations are rejected along with manual routing/redemption. Removing personal preferences requires no schema change; rollback restores their previous effect. Schema 12 requires the reviewed [migration and rollback procedure](automatic-routing-migration.md).

## Failure behavior

Explicit quota rejection creates durable cooldown. Before a new generation, an owned HTTP/compact/WS continuation can select an eligible alternative with its full current context. An existing downstream WS stays open while its upstream connection is replaced. The latest completed input/output window reconstructs incremental `previous_response_id` requests; complete tool items and encrypted context pass unchanged. Account-specific `x-codex-turn-state` is dropped on transfer, while scoped session/thread/cache identities remain stable. The new account's native rate-limit events are forwarded without pooling percentages.

A fresh live limits check can release a stale subscription-reset cooldown when upstream explicitly reports `allowed: true` with valid general-meter windows. Percentages alone never release it. Concurrent quota rejections or newer stored windows for that account defer recovery to a later refresh; Retry-After and operational pauses remain intact. No reset credit is consumed by this recovery. Deployment requires the reviewed [schema 10 to 11 migration](cooldown-recovery-migration.md).

A handshake 429 precedes inference. An HTTP 429 body or WS `error` with a recognized quota code may also permit another account only before generation acceptance: no response ID, usage, `response.created`, output or uncertain event may have been observed. A request considers at most four quota-refused inference attempts; each replacement handshake considers at most four accounts. Each refused attempt has its own durable record with unknown counters. All eligible alternatives cooling down returns a quota error with retry information.

A known idle WebSocket closure permits a replacement handshake on the same account for a separate next operation, after authorization and eligibility checks. Recover only the latest completed window or accept full input; never submit old-socket previous IDs to a replacement connection.

Large full generations may select upstream HTTP before submission without changing account affinity or the client's WS protocol. HTTP continuations cannot reference upstream WS response IDs. Expand the same bounded recovery window or reject locally with `previous_response_not_found`, allowing the client to reconstruct full input without an inference attempt or usage row. A full-context retry/reconnect still validates the current bearer, context proofs, account policy and availability. Transport selection never authorizes account failover or replay of an ambiguous inference.

Recovery retains at most 16 MiB per current window under a shared 64 MiB representation budget, with 16,384 input/output items per WS. Small windows retain parsed input with serialized-byte accounting; larger windows use in-memory zstd and charge their encoded buffer capacity. Decoded recovery is separately bounded to 64 MiB, with an 8 MiB codec history window; exceeding this optional recovery capacity does not reject a complete inference input. Completed-item assembly remains bounded to 1 MiB. Ordinary operation can continue on its original native upstream socket without a snapshot. HTTP-backed incremental input requires the latest recoverable window; unavailable/older HTTP history returns local `previous_response_not_found` for client-controlled full input, while an unrecoverable native quota transfer returns `context_recovery_unavailable`. Older response branches require a full input on transfer. History is never written to disk and disappears when the connection closes or the router restarts. No compression worker, unbounded history cache or inference replay is introduced.

Temporary catalog/refresh/Responses failures produce operation-specific durable backoff: 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160, then 300 seconds, extended by valid Retry-After. A new operation after expiry is the probe; there are no background probes. Any current pause excludes new operations. Known pauses return upstream_backoff and retry information, retaining account affinity.

An upstream WS close with code 1009 is a request-specific message-size rejection, not an unavailable account. It returns `upstream_request_too_large` without publishing operational backoff, replaying the request or moving accounts. Other sessions on that account remain eligible. Its interrupted accounting still retains unknown usage when no counters were observed.

Ordering and credential generation prevent late successes/failures from overwriting newer observations. Catalog success does not clear a Responses failure. Refresh release and pause publication are atomic, including across multiple workers.

Upstream authentication failure is redacted as upstream_authentication_error (HTTP 502 or a started stream error), distinct from the client's invalid bearer. Native WS closes. A separate next operation performs leased refresh. Repeated rejection of the refreshed generation requires operator reauth. Handshake 401/429 never triggers Chat HTTP fallback.

## Opaque context

The router returns an authenticated `exrctx1` envelope around `encrypted_content`, each `encrypted_function_args` element, issued turn state and saved conversation IDs. Empty encrypted argument arrays remain valid. Encrypted metadata identifies the router user and issuing account; the exact upstream value and field kind are authenticated. Internal account IDs are not exposed. The original upstream value is extracted only in transient memory before dispatch. Clients must preserve these opaque values exactly.

Normal output creates **no context-binding rows**. Ownership proofs have no router-imposed time limit and survive restart with the existing HMAC key and user/account state. This includes previously issued proofs whose legacy 24-hour expiry has passed. The authenticated legacy expiry field remains in the wire format but is no longer an authorization condition; new envelopes use the maximum signed 64-bit value for compatibility with older readers. Added/done/completed events for the same value carry identical proofs. Incoming opaque snapshots retain the bound of 16,384 distinct elements. Proofs remain usable when the transfer registry is full. This does not guarantee that an upstream-owned conversation or turn state remains available indefinitely.

Raw values without a router proof and foreign/tampered proofs return `context_not_found` before inference. Valid proofs retain account affinity, subject to current bearer validity, account authorization and eligibility on every request. Revoked tokens and disabled accounts are not reauthorized by an old proof. Conflicting untransferred owners or an incompatible bound WS return `context_account_mismatch`. A different bearer belonging to the same user can continue. Context imported from clients bypassing this router remains unsupported.

Only authenticated envelopes are accepted, even if a raw value still has a stored digest. At service startup, unpaired records from the former per-output registry are removed. Paired transfer records are retained regardless of legacy expiry, and their stored expiry is normalized to the maximum signed 64-bit value. No schema or key migration is required.

Quota and automatic soft transfers still require complete current context. **New transfers never insert or update context-binding rows.** After checking the selected account's active credential generation, the router issues a client-carried `exrtr1` receipt authenticating the user, destination and exact field-specific keyed digests made portable by that transfer. A receipt cannot authorize unsigned/foreign context or unrelated mixed-account history. Portable ancestors can be shared by concurrent forks; newer untransferred output retains its issuing account. Repeated transfers prove only newly portable values; an empty receipt can update the routing hint without duplicating previous digest lists.

Receipts use empty `reasoning` items with opaque `encrypted_content`, which Responses clients already preserve. They are emitted as added/done events immediately after `response.created`, with consistent output indices and assembled JSON/terminal output when provided. They contain no model-generated text. Clients must preserve these items along with the rest of their opaque history. The router authenticates and removes them before upstream dispatch; they never reach OpenAI. A proven retired turn token is authenticated and discarded before owner comparison, including after reconnect or restart.

Transfer proof size is bounded by the **current** snapshot of 16,384 unique opaque values, not lifetime activity. Each digest is 32 bytes inside authenticated encryption; at most four receipts are issued during one request's bounded account attempts, under a separate 1 MiB routing-output assembly budget. Existing request, concurrency and WS recovery budgets remain unchanged. Receipt parsing is bounded by the request size and at most 16,384 receipt items; only digest matches in the current input are retained in memory. Compaction replaces the old context/receipts with its one ordinary account-bound checkpoint; it does not require or emit a separate transfer receipt.

Legacy paired digest overrides remain readable and survive restart regardless of their old expiry. Their existing finite registry is not enlarged by new sessions or transfers. A full legacy registry no longer prevents a new transfer. No age-based eviction of legacy records invalidates saved forks. Raw values without authenticated envelopes remain unsupported even if their digests are present.

### Saved conversations

Saved backend conversation IDs and `x-codex-turn-state` also carry user/account proofs. The router unwraps IDs and turn state before forwarding. Saved conversations stay pinned to their issuing account and cannot migrate because their backend-owned history is unavailable. Raw IDs and state are not persisted.

### Upgrade and rollback

Deploy server, native gateway and standalone binaries built from the same source. Existing client-held proofs, including legacy-expired proofs, continue to work on upgrade. Already-deleted transfer markers cannot be reconstructed from an envelope; a mixed-account history whose markers were pruned by an older binary may still require full text/tool recovery. Raw context issued by a pre-proof binary is no longer accepted: start a new session with full text/tool history. Preserve the HMAC key that authenticates client-carried proofs.

The ownership-envelope and database schema are unchanged; the transfer-receipt format is new. Startup normalization preserves existing paired transfer records. **Older binaries cannot read `exrtr1` receipts**: rolling back requires reconnecting with full text/tool history without these router-only carriers. Rolling back also restores that binary's 24-hour check for previously issued legacy ownership proofs. A pre-proof binary cannot accept ownership envelopes either. A database backup alone cannot make old binaries understand client-held proofs. Do not strip or silently accept unverified envelopes as a fallback.

## Operator and diagnostics

Operators use local `exrd admin oauth add --device`, list, reauth and disable. Remote clients never receive upstream account IDs or credentials. Doctor shows pool health counts and email-labeled per-account quota summaries; `server.quota` retains the older single-account-only field. `exr limits`/Overview TUI show actual 8-hour/weekly/other reported windows with freshness and resets.

Recorded two-account routing, checkpoint portability and synthetic quota-switch evidence is in [live testing](live-testing.md#cross-account-continuity-and-synthetic-quota-failover-2026-10-02). Actual external quota/authentication outages, live WS encrypted-tool portability, full context and sustained concurrent failover remain unverified.

## Starting an inactive weekly window

Live limits may activate an apparently unused weekly window once with a minimal `gpt-5.6-sol` request. Eligibility requires fresh 100% remaining, a reported 10080-minute duration, and a reset exactly seven days ahead at minute precision. Selection is pinned to the account and respects operator policy, catalog support, quota cooldown and operational backoff. A durable claim prevents another attempt for seven days, including after an uncertain result or restart. This heuristic is separate from reset credits and never consumes them. See [CLI behavior](cli.md#weekly-activation) and [schema migration](quota-activation-migration.md).
